Iron salts, silver, egg white, plant juice, and sunlight. Walk into Atelier Square, where every early process has its own atelier. Step through a door, take up the bench, and print your photograph the way it was printed in 1842. Other apprentices may be working beside you.

    The Master of the studio

    So you are the new apprentice. Good; the last one ran off with a tintypist. The rules of this house are few. Work in dim light. Coat what you mean to print. Take only the sun you need, then wash.

    Nine ateliers stand round this square, one for each process, each with its own chemistry and its own light: a sun yard for the cyanotype, a dark plate room for the daguerreotype, a glasshouse for the plant prints. Letters arrive from people who want things made. Bring me a print worth keeping and I will press the house seal beside it in your ledger. You will find me outside my office on the north side of the square.
